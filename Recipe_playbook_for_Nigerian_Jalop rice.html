Nigerian Jollof Rice

West Africa
  • Prep20 min
  • Cook50 min
  • Serves6
  • DifficultyModerate
Ingredients
IngredientAmount
Long-grain parboiled rice3 cups
Fresh tomatoes, blended6 medium
Red bell peppers (tatashe), blended2
Scotch bonnet pepper, blended1 (to taste)
Onions, chopped2 large
Vegetable oil1/2 cup
Tomato paste2 tbsp
Chicken or beef stock3 cups
Bay leaves2
Curry powder1 tsp
Dried thyme1 tsp
Salt & seasoning cubesTo taste
Method
  1. Blend the tomatoes, bell peppers, and scotch bonnet into a smooth puree. Set aside.
  2. Heat the oil in a heavy pot and fry half the chopped onions until soft and translucent.
  3. Stir in the tomato paste and fry for 3–4 minutes to remove the raw taste, then add the blended pepper mixture. Cook down on medium heat for 15–20 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens and the oil begins to separate at the edges.
  4. Add the stock, bay leaves, curry powder, thyme, and seasoning. Taste and adjust the salt.
  5. Wash the rice and stir it into the sauce, making sure every grain is coated. The liquid should sit just above the level of the rice.
  6. Cover the pot tightly (a layer of foil under the lid helps trap the steam) and cook on low heat for 30–35 minutes, without stirring, until the rice is tender and the classic smoky bottom layer has formed.
  7. Fluff gently with a fork and serve hot, traditionally alongside fried plantain, coleslaw, or grilled chicken.
Tip: Letting the pepper base cook down properly is what gives jollof its deep color and flavor — don't rush this step.

Suya (Spiced Beef Skewers)

Northern Nigeria / Hausa
  • Prep25 min + 1 hr marinate
  • Cook15 min
  • Serves4
  • DifficultyEasy
Ingredients
IngredientAmount
Beef sirloin, thinly sliced700 g
Roasted peanuts, finely ground1 cup
Ground ginger1 tbsp
Ground cayenne pepper1–2 tbsp
Paprika1 tbsp
Onion powder1 tsp
Stock cube, crushed1
Vegetable oil3 tbsp
Salt1 tsp
Onion & tomato, sliced (for serving)1 each
Method
  1. Combine the ground peanuts, ginger, cayenne, paprika, onion powder, crushed stock cube, and salt — this spice mix is known as yaji.
  2. Thread the thin beef slices onto skewers, brush lightly with oil, then coat generously with the yaji spice mix on both sides.
  3. Let the skewers rest for at least an hour so the spice penetrates the meat (overnight in the fridge is even better).
  4. Grill over hot coals or under a broiler, turning frequently, for 10–15 minutes until the meat is charred at the edges and cooked through.
  5. Dust with a little extra yaji before serving, alongside sliced raw onion and tomato.
Tip: No grill? A hot cast-iron griddle pan gets you most of the way to authentic char.

Spaghetti alla Carbonara

Rome, Italy
  • Prep10 min
  • Cook20 min
  • Serves4
  • DifficultyModerate
Ingredients
IngredientAmount
Spaghetti400 g
Guanciale or pancetta, diced150 g
Large egg yolks4
Whole egg1
Pecorino Romano, finely grated1 cup
Black pepper, freshly cracked1–2 tsp
Salt (for pasta water)To taste
Method
  1. Bring a large pot of well-salted water to a boil and cook the spaghetti until just shy of al dente.
  2. While the pasta cooks, render the guanciale in a cold pan over medium heat until crisp and golden. Turn off the heat.
  3. In a bowl, whisk the egg yolks, whole egg, grated pecorino, and a generous amount of black pepper into a thick paste.
  4. Just before draining the pasta, reserve a cup of the starchy pasta water.
  5. Add the drained pasta directly to the pan with the guanciale, tossing to coat in the rendered fat off the heat.
  6. Pour in the egg mixture, tossing quickly and constantly, adding splashes of pasta water as needed, until the sauce turns silky and coats the strands — the residual heat cooks the eggs without scrambling them.
  7. Serve immediately with extra pecorino and black pepper on top.
Tip: Keep the pan off direct heat when adding the egg mixture — this is the single most important step to avoid scrambled eggs.

Fluffy Buttermilk Pancakes

North America
  • Prep10 min
  • Cook15 min
  • Serves4 (about 12 pancakes)
  • DifficultyEasy
Ingredients
IngredientAmount
All-purpose flour2 cups
Sugar2 tbsp
Baking powder2 tsp
Baking soda1/2 tsp
Salt1/2 tsp
Buttermilk2 cups
Large eggs2
Unsalted butter, melted3 tbsp
Vanilla extract1 tsp
Method
  1. Whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt in a large bowl.
  2. In a separate bowl, whisk the buttermilk, eggs, melted butter, and vanilla.
  3. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry and stir gently just until combined — a few lumps are fine, and overmixing leads to tough pancakes.
  4. Let the batter rest for 5 minutes while a griddle or non-stick pan heats over medium heat.
  5. Lightly grease the pan and pour about 1/4 cup of batter per pancake.
  6. Cook until bubbles form across the surface and the edges look set, about 2–3 minutes, then flip and cook for another 1–2 minutes until golden.
  7. Serve warm, stacked, with butter and maple syrup.
Tip: Resting the batter gives the baking powder time to activate, which is a big part of getting that fluffy rise.

Classic Chocolate Chip Cookies

United States
  • Prep15 min + 30 min chill
  • Cook12 min per batch
  • ServesMakes about 24 cookies
  • DifficultyEasy
Ingredients
IngredientAmount
Unsalted butter, softened1 cup
Brown sugar, packed3/4 cup
Granulated sugar3/4 cup
Large eggs2
Vanilla extract1 tsp
All-purpose flour2 1/4 cups
Baking soda1 tsp
Salt1 tsp
Semi-sweet chocolate chips2 cups
Method
  1. Cream the butter with the brown and granulated sugar until light and fluffy, about 2–3 minutes.
  2. Beat in the eggs one at a time, then stir in the vanilla.
  3. In a separate bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, and salt, then fold into the wet mixture until just combined.
  4. Fold in the chocolate chips.
  5. Chill the dough for at least 30 minutes — this keeps the cookies from spreading too thin.
  6. Scoop rounded tablespoons of dough onto a lined baking sheet, spaced well apart, and bake at 190°C (375°F) for 10–12 minutes, until the edges are golden but the centers still look slightly underdone.
  7. Cool on the tray for 5 minutes before transferring to a wire rack — they'll finish setting as they cool.
Tip: Pulling the cookies out while the centers still look a touch underbaked is the secret to that soft, chewy middle.
