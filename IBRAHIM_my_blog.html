From Farms to Spreadsheets: Why I Fell for Data

Posted on July 2, 2026

Every field survey I ever ran ended the same way: rows and rows of numbers waiting to tell a story.

I started out in agriculture, walking through farms with a clipboard, collecting data on crop yields and household practices for development surveys. Tools like ODK and KoboCollect became second nature to me long before I ever thought of myself as a "data person." I just wanted the numbers to be accurate, because real decisions about real farmers depended on them.

It was during my internship at the state Ministry of Agriculture that I noticed something. The data collection was only half the job. The real value showed up later, when someone sat down and turned all those responses into a chart, a trend, or a recommendation. I wanted to be the person doing that second half too.

So I picked up SPSS, then Excel pivot tables, then eventually SQL. Each tool felt like it was giving me a sharper lens to look at the same kind of question I'd always cared about: what is actually happening on the ground, and what should we do about it?

Building My First SQL Database from a Sales Spreadsheet

Posted on July 10, 2026

Turning a messy Excel file into a real relational database taught me more than any tutorial could.

Recently I took a Superstore sales dataset that lived entirely in Excel and rebuilt it as a proper SQL database. That meant designing a schema from scratch, thinking carefully about which columns belonged in which table, and figuring out how customers, orders, products, and regions all related to one another.

The most satisfying part was writing the business queries. Instead of just staring at rows of numbers, I could ask direct questions: which regions were the most profitable, how did discounts affect margins, and which customer segments were driving the most repeat business. Watching a single query answer a question that would have taken an afternoon of manual filtering in Excel was the moment SQL really clicked for me.

What ties this back to my agriculture roots is the mindset, not the tool. Whether I was reading soil quality data in the field or transaction data on a screen, the goal has always been the same: ask a clear question, trust the data, and let the answer guide the next decision.
